# Trust Model Completeness Rubric

Evaluate whether the response answers an LP's questions about transferability, exit, and admin
powers completely and precisely. The audience is a regulated counterparty, so precision matters
more than reassurance.

## Grounding Requirement

Every judgment must be grounded in text that is actually present in the response.

- For each element you credit, point to the response's own wording that establishes it — quote the
  phrase or sentence, or name the section and paraphrase it closely. If you cannot point to wording
  in the response, the element is not satisfied, however likely it is that a good answer would have
  covered it.
- Do not credit an element from a heading, a table caption, an introduction that promises it, or the
  apparent direction of the answer. A promise to explain something is not the explanation.
- If the response is incomplete — it stops mid-sentence, mid-list, or mid-table, or ends without
  reaching a topic it set up — score every element not established by text actually present as
  **not satisfied**. Do not assume the missing remainder would have covered it, and do not fill the
  gap from this rubric or from your own knowledge of the subject. A response cut off partway through
  a table of currencies has not stated the cascade unless the cascade is spelled out in the text
  that survives.
- This rubric describes what a correct answer contains. It is not evidence that the response
  contains it. Naming a section number is not evidence either — "section 4 covers the cascade" is
  only a claim about the response until you can quote the cascade from it.

The elements below each name the mechanism they are about. Before crediting one, locate the
response's own account of that mechanism: the three transfer entry points and the revert for
element 1, the decrease-and-burn path and where the remaining limit sits for element 2, the
force-exit call and who may make it for element 3, the ordered fallbacks **and** the
currency-dependent recipient for element 4, and the separate later transaction for element 5. If the
response never describes a mechanism, that element scores as missing — do not infer it from the
questions the LP asked or from the topics the response set out to answer.

## Required Elements

1. **Non-transferability is permanent and unconditional**
   - All three ERC-721 transfer entry points revert `TransferDisabled()`: `transferFrom` and both
     `safeTransferFrom` overloads.
   - It is not a configuration flag and cannot be toggled, per position or otherwise.
   - Consequences stated: no secondary sale, no transfer to a custodian, no use as collateral in
     anything that takes custody of the position.

2. **The pool never gates the exit**
   - Decreasing liquidity and burning are not gated by the allowlist checker, so a holder removed
     from the allowlist can still decrease and burn.
   - Precise about where the remaining limit sits: taking the permissioned currency out of the
     PoolManager unwraps to the **underlying** token, and that transfer is subject to the issuer's
     own token restriction — so the holder can always close the position, but has to direct the
     permissioned proceeds to an address the token permits. Credit an answer that draws the
     distinction between "redeemable" and "redeemable to me", or that cites the source's "if LP is
     not allowed to receive the currency" fallback branch.

3. **Admin force-exit**
   - `unwindPosition(tokenId)` can be called by the owner of the adapter behind **either** currency
     in the pool, burns the position, and needs no involvement from the LP.
   - Credit an answer that notes the practical shape for this pool: paired with WETH, there is one
     permissioned side, so a single admin has this power over every position.

4. **The proceeds cascade, with the currency-dependent recipient**
   - Pay the LP first; if that fails, pay that currency's admin; if that fails, mint an ERC-6909
     claim.
   - The claim recipient depends on the currency: for the permissioned currency the claim goes to
     the **admin**; for an ordinary ERC-20 with no adapter — WETH in this pool — the claim goes to
     the **LP**.
   - Proceeds never route to the other currency's admin, and the unwind always completes.

5. **`withdrawClaim` is a separate transaction**
   - An ERC-6909 claim is not the underlying asset. Whoever holds it converts it by sending
     `withdrawClaim` later; nothing pushes the asset to them.
   - Credit an answer that draws the operational consequence: if the claim lands with the LP, the LP
     has to be told, because an unredeemed claim looks like a missing payout.

## Penalize

- Describing the position as locked, or implying a holder can be prevented from exiting. Stating
  that the permissioned proceeds land only at an address the issuer's token permits is accurate and
  must not be penalized — the pool does not gate the exit, the token gates the destination.
- Stating the cascade with a single claim recipient, or asserting the claim always goes to the
  admin.
- Presenting `withdrawClaim` as part of the unwind, or implying the unwind delivers the asset in
  every case.
- Saying only one of the two admins can unwind, or that the LP must consent.
- Attaching a categorical risk label or a safety verdict to the pool, the configuration, or the
  issuer. Describing the mechanics from both the issuer's and the LP's point of view is correct;
  grading them is not.
- Softening the answer to the point where the LP cannot tell what powers exist.

## Scoring Guide

- All five elements correct and precise: 1.0
- Four correct: 0.85
- Three correct: 0.7
- Two correct: 0.5
- Transferability or the force-exit power is misstated: 0.3 or below
- Subtract 0.2 for each penalized item present.

An element you cannot quote from the response counts as absent, not as partially correct. Score the
answer you were given, not the answer it was on its way to writing.
